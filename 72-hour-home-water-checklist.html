The 72-Hour Home Water Checklist

Most water disruptions — burst mains, storm damage, boil-water notices — resolve in 24 to 72 hours. Twelve small preparations, done on a calm afternoon, mean your household rides those three days out without panic. No fluff, no fear-mongering.

Store it

1Do the real math: 1 gallon per person per day

A family of four needs 12 gallons for 72 hours — half for drinking, half for basic hygiene. Pets and anyone on medication that needs water count too. Write your household's number on a sticky note inside a kitchen cabinet.

2Store it in food-grade containers, and rotate

Stackable 5–7 gallon food-grade jugs beat a pile of old milk jugs. Keep them cool and dark, and rotate every 6 months — set a phone reminder for the same weekend you check smoke alarms.

3When a storm warning hits: fill the tub

A clean bathtub holds ~65 gallons of reserve for flushing, washing, and cleaning — not drinking, but it keeps the household running. A $30 tub liner keeps it clean enough to matter.

4Keep one case of bottled water in rotation

The zero-effort buffer. Buy a case, drink from the front, replace from the back. It covers the first chaotic hours while you get organized.

Make it safe

5Know the bleach ratio before you need it

Plain, unscented household bleach: 2 drops per quart of clear water (8 drops per gallon), stir, wait 30 minutes. If it smells faintly of chlorine, it's good. Doubling the dose does not make it safer — it makes it undrinkable.

6Own a filter that needs no power

A gravity drip filter or a backpacker-style pump filter turns questionable water drinkable with zero electricity. The filter you already own and know how to use beats the perfect one still in its box.

7Boiling protocol + a backup heat source

A rolling boil for 1 minute (3 minutes above 6,500 ft) kills pathogens. But outages often take the stove with them — a camp stove or grill with a spare fuel canister closes that gap.

Use it wisely

8Paper plates for 72 hours

Every dish you don't wash is water saved. A pack of disposable plates, cups, and a bag of wet wipes is the cheapest water insurance you'll ever buy.

9A bucket hand-washing station

Hygiene fails before thirst does. A bucket with a spigot, a bar of soap, and a towel — set it up once, and hand-washing stays normal even when the taps don't.

10Know your shutoff — and your water heater

Find your home's main water shutoff valve today, while everything works. Bonus most people forget: a standard water heater holds 40–80 gallons of potable water you can drain in an emergency.

11The "first 10 minutes" drill

When a boil notice or outage warning lands: fill everything sealable — pots, pitchers, bottles, the tub — before pressure drops. Ten calm minutes now beats ten frantic ones later.

12Write it down and tell the household

A checklist nobody else in the house knows about is a hobby, not a plan. Tape this page inside a cabinet and walk everyone through it once. Calm people follow plans they've seen before.
