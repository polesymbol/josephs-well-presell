The 72-Hour Home Water Checklist

Twelve things to have ready before the tap runs dry — because the middle of an outage is the worst time to discover what you’re missing. Work through it once, on a calm day.

Store it

  • 1. Your 3-day supply, actually in the house. One gallon per person per day for drinking and basic sanitation — three days minimum, and count the pets.Bottled cases, filled food-grade containers, or both. A plan to buy water later is not storage.

  • 2. Containers you can pour from. At least one container with a spigot or a clean pitcher with a lid.A 5-gallon jug you can’t pour from without spilling half of it is a problem on day two.

  • 3. Your hidden reserves, mapped. Know where your main shutoff is, and how to drain your water heater — it typically holds 30–50 gallons.Write both down. In a real outage, nobody remembers a YouTube video.

Make it safe

  • 4. A filter you trust — and a spare element. A gravity or pump filter rated for bacteria and protozoa, tested once on ordinary tap water.Filters handle most things. They are not the same as purifiers — know which one you own.

  • 5. A purification backup. Unscented household bleach (standard 6%: 8 drops per gallon of clear water, stir, wait 30 minutes) or purification tablets, with the instructions kept with them.Bleach loses strength with age — replace the bottle yearly. If water is cloudy, filter it through cloth first and double the dose.

  • 6. A way to boil, without the grid. Camp stove, grill, or rocket stove, plus fuel for three days.A rolling boil for one minute makes most water safe (three minutes above 6,500 ft). Fuel you don’t have is the usual failure point.

  • 7. Electrolytes. Oral rehydration salts or electrolyte mix — a small box, in date.In any water emergency, dehydration is the real danger, especially for children and older family members.

Live with it

  • 8. A sanitation kit. A bucket with a tight lid, heavy-duty bags, hand sanitizer, and wet wipes.Toilets stop flushing long before they stop being needed. This is the item everyone forgets and everyone regrets.

  • 9. Non-drinking water, kept separate. Rain barrel, pool, or stored grey water for flushing and cleaning — labelled, and never mixed up with drinking water.Every gallon of non-drinking water you use for flushing is a gallon of drinking water saved.

  • 10. Special needs, covered in writing. Extra water for infant formula, medications that need water, medical devices, and anyone who can’t queue or carry.Go name by name through your household. The generic checklist ends here — yours shouldn’t.

Keep it true

  • 11. Rotation dates on the calendar. Stored water refreshed every 6 months, bleach replaced yearly, filter elements checked, fuel topped up.Preparedness expires quietly. Dates are what keep this list true in a year.

  • 12. The weekend test. Pick one ordinary weekend: shut off the main, and live on your setup for a day.You will find two or three gaps — everyone does. Finding them on a Saturday is the entire point. An untested system is a hope, not a plan.
